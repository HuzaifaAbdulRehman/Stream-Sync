In the crumbling heart of the old city, there was a shop that repaired time itself.

Hidden behind ivy-choked walls and a crooked oak door, the Clockmaker’s shop ticked and tocked and hummed with secrets. Every clock inside was strange — they didn’t just tell time. Some whispered forgotten songs, some replayed moments from a different life. Others... others kept things locked away.

Mara was the Clockmaker’s new apprentice. Barely sixteen, clever with her hands but stubborn in her heart. She knew the city was dying — streets flooded when it rained, bricks crumbled like sand, and the sky was never truly blue anymore. But the Clockmaker insisted the city could be saved, if she just learned fast enough.

"Not all time is meant to move forward," he told her one evening, adjusting his silver spectacles. "Sometimes you must wind it back."

Mara spent her days polishing gears and carving tiny bones into cogs. Her nights were spent listening to the clocks whisper. She began to hear things: the city’s laughter from a hundred years ago, the sobbing of a man who lost his fortune, the thunder of rebellion yet to come.

One night, she found it — a clock made of black iron, hidden beneath a rotting tarpaulin. It had no hands. No numbers. Only a keyhole at its center, and a single word etched in ancient script: Remedy.

Compelled by a force she didn’t understand, Mara stole the master key from the Clockmaker’s belt and wound the iron clock.

Instantly, the shop shuddered. The walls faded. Mara found herself standing in the same city — but vibrant, breathing, alive. Children raced past her, their laughter clear. Trees crowned the streets. The clock towers chimed a golden song.

She stumbled back into the shop, heart racing, only to find the Clockmaker waiting. His eyes were sad.

"I told you," he said quietly. "Some clocks... are meant to stay broken."

Before she could answer, the iron clock spun wildly. The world around them twisted into shadow and ash. Mara screamed, but it was too late.

The city had been given another chance — but it came at a terrible price.

And Mara... was now the new Clockmaker, trapped for centuries, repairing the mistakes of time.
