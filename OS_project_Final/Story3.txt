Detective Aria Cole didn’t believe in ghosts — but the letter in her mailbox was impossible to explain.

It was a cold autumn morning when she found it, tucked between bills and flyers. An old envelope, yellowed at the edges, addressed in perfect handwriting:

"To Aria Cole. Solve it."

Inside was a single sheet of paper. On it, the name: Eliza Wren.

Aria’s chest tightened. Eliza Wren had been her first case, back when she was still green, eager, foolish. A missing girl, seventeen years old. No leads. No closure.

Aria never found her. No one did.

And now, twenty years later, someone was dragging the past back.

She followed the trail the letter set out. It wasn't easy — old addresses led to burnt-down houses, witnesses were dead or disappeared. But someone — or something — was leaving her clues. An old locket buried in the ruins of Eliza’s family home. A diary hidden in the rafters of the abandoned school.

Each piece painted a darker picture: Eliza hadn’t run away. She’d been taken, hidden, by someone in plain sight.

The final clue brought Aria to the docks, where the river smothered the city's edges in mist. There, half-buried in mud, she found another letter. Sealed with wax. The same handwriting.

"Thank you for not forgetting me."

As Aria read, she realized the truth: Eliza had been buried alive under the old pier, sealed away by someone powerful — someone Aria had trusted. Chief Merrick, her old mentor.

It shattered her.

But it also freed her.

With trembling hands, Aria made the call. Merrick was arrested. The city buzzed with scandal.

Eliza's body was recovered and finally laid to rest.

And as Aria stood at her grave, she swore she heard a whisper on the breeze.

"Thank you."

She didn’t believe in ghosts. But some promises, she realized, were too strong to die.
